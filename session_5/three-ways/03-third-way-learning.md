# The Third Way: Continual learning and experimentation

Pair 3 · Session 5 · "Explain a Way in three minutes"

## Step 1. What Claude would say

**The idea.** The Third Way is about culture. It creates an environment where people experiment continually, take risks, and learn from both success and failure. It also recognizes that repetition and practice are the way to mastery. What one person learns becomes team knowledge, and then knowledge for the whole organization.

**How to get there.** Treat accidents and failures as chances to learn, not to punish (a "just culture"). Turn local discoveries into global improvements by sharing them. Put resilience into daily work, even by creating small failures on purpose. Set aside time to improve the work itself. Leaders show that learning matters.

**Example from Café Cumbre.**

- After an incident, the team writes a blameless postmortem and records what to change.
- A lesson from one pull request, such as a common mistake in branch names, becomes a rule in the repository's guide for everyone.
- A Game Day: the team breaks the café's API on purpose in a safe environment to see how it recovers.
- A skill that saves time for one person is shared in the repository so the whole group can use it.

**One sentence to remember.** Learning means: it is safe to try, safe to fail, and what we learn is shared.

## Step 2. Check against the official slides (64 to 76)

**Where to look.** Slides 74 and 75.

**What the slides say, in short.**

- Slide 74 presents the Third Way: a culture of continual experimentation (which requires taking risks and learning from success and failure) and the idea that repetition and practice are a prerequisite for mastery. It contrasts the three Ways: the First Way is work flowing left to right, the Second Way is fast feedback right to left, and the Third Way is a culture of continual learning.
- Slide 75 lists the five capabilities of the Third Way.

**The five capabilities** (the order may differ from this one):

1. Institutionalize the improvement of daily work
2. Enable organizational learning and a safe culture
3. Convert local discoveries into global improvements
4. Inject resilience patterns into our daily work
5. Leaders reinforce a learning culture

**Compare with Step 1.**

- Did the explanation say that this Way is about **culture**, and not about a tool?
- Did it connect individual learning to team and organization learning?
- Did it mention the **role of leaders**? It is easy to forget.
- Is a safe culture (no blame) clearly part of the answer? The official material treats a fair response to accidents as a prerequisite for learning.

**Watch out for.** Chaos engineering and Game Days are examples of injecting resilience, but they are not the whole Third Way. The Way is the culture that makes them possible.
